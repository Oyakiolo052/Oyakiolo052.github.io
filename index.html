Md Shamimur Rahman

PhD in Computer Science. Incoming PostDoctoral Fellow, Polytechnique Montréal, Canada.

Room 254

176 Thorvaldson Building

110 Science Place, University of Saskatchewan

Saskatoon, Saskatchewan

Md Shamimur Rahman completed his Ph.D. in Computer Science at the University of Saskatchewan, Canada, in 2026, conducting his doctoral research in the Software Research (SR) Lab under the supervision of Prof. Chanchal K. Roy and Dr. Zadia Codabux. His Ph.D. dissertation was titled “Beyond the Quality Gate: Understanding and Improving Developer Satisfaction for Sustainable Code Review”. He also served as a Sessional Lecturer at the University of Saskatchewan. Previously, he was a Lecturer and later an Assistant Professor in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at Khulna University of Engineering & Technology (KUET), Bangladesh.

Previously, he completed his master’s at the University of Saskatchewan, Canada, under the supervision of Prof. Chanchal Roy. His master’s thesis title was “Aspect of Code Cloning Towards Software Bug and Imminent Maintenance: A Perspective on Open-source and Industrial Mobile Applications”. Prior to this, Shamimur completed his bachelor’s degree from the Department of Computer Science and Engineering of Khulna University of Engineering & Technology (KUET), Bangladesh.

Shamimur’s research lies at the intersection of Software Engineering and Artificial Intelligence, with a focus on empirical software engineering, human-centered software development, and trustworthy AI-assisted software engineering. His research combines software repository mining, empirical studies, and data-driven methods to understand and improve software engineering practices, particularly modern code review. He investigates both technical and human aspects of software development, including developer satisfaction, communication, collaboration, and team dynamics, while developing practical insights and automated tools to support developers. His current research explores how AI-generated software can be made understandable, reviewable, trustworthy, and accountable as AI coding agents become increasingly involved in software development.
