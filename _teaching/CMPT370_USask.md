  <section class="course-section" id="overview">
    <h2>Course Overview</h2>

    <p>
      CMPT 370 introduces the principles and techniques
      of software engineering, combining theoretical
      foundations with practical experience in developing
      a medium-sized software system as part of a team.
    </p>

    <p>
      The course covers software development processes,
      Agile methodologies, requirements engineering,
      user stories, software design, architecture,
      quality assurance, testing, technical debt,
      refactoring, and software maintenance.
    </p>

    <p>
      A central component is a semester-long group
      project in which students apply software engineering
      practices to plan, design, implement, test, and
      demonstrate a working software product.
      The course emphasizes not only technical
      knowledge but also collaboration, communication,
      project management, and professional responsibility.
    </p>

    <p>
      The course introduces major software engineering
      activities across the development lifecycle,

        responsibilities, and engineering practices
        for developing software systems.
      </p>

      <h3>
        <i class="fa-solid fa-arrows-rotate"></i>

        <i class="fa-solid fa-list-check"></i>

    <h2>Teaching and Learning Approach</h2>

    <p>
      The course combines theoretical instruction,
      practical software engineering examples,
      collaborative activities, and a semester-long
      group development project.
    </p>

    <div class="course-methods">

      <div>
        <i class="fa-solid fa-chalkboard-user"></i>
        <h3>Lectures</h3>
        <p>
          In-person lectures introduce software
          engineering principles and techniques,
          supported by practical examples
          and classroom discussions.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <i class="fa-solid fa-people-group"></i>
        <h3>Team-Based Development</h3>
        <p>
          Students collaborate in teams of five
          to develop a medium-sized software
          product throughout the semester.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <i class="fa-solid fa-arrows-rotate"></i>
        <h3>Scrum-Style Tutorials</h3>
        <p>
          Weekly tutorial meetings allow teams
          to report progress, discuss obstacles,
          review deliverables, and receive
          guidance from teaching assistants.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <i class="fa-solid fa-list-check"></i>
        <h3>Project Milestones</h3>
        <p>
          Project deliverables encourage students
          to apply requirements engineering,
          software design, implementation,
          testing, and documentation practices.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <i class="fa-solid fa-presentation-screen"></i>
        <h3>Project Demonstrations</h3>
        <p>
          Teams present their completed software
          products, answer technical questions,
          and demonstrate their implementations.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <i class="fa-solid fa-comments"></i>
        <h3>Feedback and Support</h3>
        <p>
          Students receive feedback through
          tutorial meetings, project presentations,
          course discussions, and instructor
          communication.
        </p>
      </div>

    </div>
  </section>

  <!-- Semester-long Group Project -->
  <section class="course-section" id="group-project">
    <h2>Semester-Long Software Development Project</h2>

    <p>
      A major component of CMPT 370 is a
      collaborative project where students
      work in teams of five to plan, design,
      implement, and demonstrate a software
      product of their choosing.
    </p>

    <p>
      The project provides practical experience
      with the challenges of real-world software
      development, including understanding
      user requirements, making design decisions,
      coordinating team members, implementing
      features, and evaluating software quality.
    </p>

    <div class="course-grid">

      <div class="course-card">
        <i class="fa-solid fa-lightbulb"></i>
        <h3>Project Planning</h3>
        <p>
          Define the product idea, identify
          potential users, establish project
          scope, and plan development activities.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="course-card">
        <i class="fa-solid fa-list-check"></i>
        <h3>Requirements and User Stories</h3>
        <p>
          Identify user needs, document
          requirements, and organize
          functionality into user stories
          and epics.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="course-card">
        <i class="fa-solid fa-sitemap"></i>
        <h3>Design and Implementation</h3>
        <p>
          Translate requirements into
          software designs, implement features,
          and integrate individual contributions
          into a working system.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="course-card">
        <i class="fa-solid fa-vial-circle-check"></i>
        <h3>Testing and Documentation</h3>
        <p>
          Test the software, document the
          implementation, and prepare
          project artifacts for evaluation.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="course-card">
        <i class="fa-solid fa-users-gear"></i>
        <h3>Team Coordination</h3>
        <p>
          Participate in weekly Scrum-style
          meetings, communicate progress,
          resolve issues, and document
          individual contributions.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="course-card">
        <i class="fa-solid fa-display"></i>
        <h3>Final Presentation and Demo</h3>
        <p>
          Present the completed product,
          explain engineering decisions,
          answer questions, and demonstrate
          the working system.
        </p>
      </div>

    </div>

    <div class="course-reflection" style="margin-top: 22px;">
      <i class="fa-solid fa-people-group"></i>

      <div>
        <p>
          <strong>Project-Based Learning</strong>
        </p>

        <p>
          The project emphasizes the practical
          application of software engineering
          concepts and the development of
          collaborative problem-solving skills.

          and documentation must work together
          to produce a coherent software system.

  <!-- Responsible Use of Generative AI -->
  <section class="course-section" id="responsible-ai">
    <h2>Responsible Use of Generative AI</h2>

    <p>
      Recognizing the increasing role of generative AI
      in software development, the course established
      guidelines for responsible and transparent use
      of AI tools in student projects.
    </p>

    <p>
      Students were permitted to use generative AI
      as a supplementary learning and brainstorming
      resource, provided that its use did not replace
      the core learning activities of software
      engineering.
    </p>

    <div class="course-grid">

      <div class="course-card">
        <i class="fa-solid fa-check-circle"></i>
        <h3>Permitted Uses</h3>
        <p>
          Brainstorming ideas, exploring alternative
          designs, understanding programming syntax,
          explaining concepts, investigating errors,
          and obtaining support for debugging
          and basic test generation.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="course-card">
        <i class="fa-solid fa-ban"></i>
        <h3>Restricted Uses</h3>
        <p>
          Generating complete solutions or significant
          portions of project logic, preparing graded
          milestones with AI, submitting code without
          understanding it, concealing AI usage, or
          substituting AI for teamwork.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="course-card">
        <i class="fa-solid fa-file-lines"></i>
        <h3>AI Usage Documentation</h3>
        <p>
          Project deliverables required an AI usage
          statement documenting when AI tools were
          used and how they supported the
          development process.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="course-card">
        <i class="fa-solid fa-user-check"></i>
        <h3>Human Accountability</h3>
        <p>
          Students remained responsible for
          understanding, validating, and explaining
          their implementations and engineering
          decisions.
        </p>
      </div>

    </div>

    <div class="course-reflection" style="margin-top: 22px;">
      <i class="fa-solid fa-lightbulb"></i>

      <div>
        <p>
          <strong>Educational Objective</strong>
        </p>

        <p>
          The objective was to help students recognize
          AI as a supporting tool while preserving
          independent technical understanding,
          engineering judgment, academic integrity,
          and meaningful team collaboration.
        </p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
